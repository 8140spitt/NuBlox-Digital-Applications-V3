# 03 — Delivery Lifecycle

## Purpose

This chapter describes how an organisation wins and delivers construction and built-environment work.

Delivery is not a separate module. It is the coordinated operation of the business functions around a client, opportunity, contract, project, property, site, programme, asset or service.

## Market, client and opportunity

NuBlox connects market intelligence, customer identity, contacts, account plans, leads, opportunities, pursuit decisions, pipeline and forecasts.

## Estimate, tender and proposal

NuBlox must support invitation/enquiry, requirements, measurement/take-off, estimate structure, resource build-ups and rates, preliminaries, risk, contingency, supplier/subcontract enquiries, tender returns, comparisons, adjudication, approvals, proposal/quotation and submission.

The Estimate is distinct from the Contract, Project WBS and Finance ledger but linked to all of them.

## Contract or appointment

Award or acceptance creates governed contractual commitments covering parties, roles, scope, clauses, obligations, key dates, pricing, payment terms, notices, change mechanisms, insurances, guarantees and approval evidence.

## Mobilisation and project setup

The delivery context establishes the project/programme, team and responsibilities, stages, WBS, work packages, cost structures, controls, information requirements, deliverable schedules, risks, procurement plan, HSE/quality plans and site/location structures.

## Design and information production

NuBlox must manage required design/information outputs, not merely store uploaded documents.

This includes requirements, design responsibilities, deliverable schedules, drawings, models, specifications, calculations, schedules, reports, submissions, reviews, comments, coordination issues, changes, revisions, issue/transmittal and acceptance.

Specialist authoring may take place in Revit, AutoCAD, Tekla, Civil 3D, Bentley products or other engineering tools while NuBlox retains the deliverable identity, requirement, responsibility, status, revision, issue and approval history.

## Planning and project controls

NuBlox coordinates WBS, schedules, baselines, milestones, dependencies, progress, resources, budget, cost plan, forecast, risks, issues, actions, decisions, changes and status reporting.

## Procurement and supply chain

The delivery requirement flows through package planning, sourcing, supplier discovery, prequalification, tender/RFQ, evaluation, negotiation, award, subcontract/PO, delivery, receipt, inventory and supplier performance.

## Construction and field execution

NuBlox supports mobilisation/access, work areas, permits, RAMS/method information, labour, plant, materials, instructions, temporary works, daily records, progress, inspections/tests, quality findings, defects, HSE events, evidence and completion/readiness.

## Commercial and financial control

Delivery produces commitments, actual costs, accruals, forecasts, applications, valuations, certificates, variations, claims, revenue, invoices, receipts/payments and final accounts.

The source commercial transaction and its Finance posting remain linked without becoming the same object.

## Quality, HSE and assurance

Assurance covers quality plans, ITPs, inspections/tests, hold/witness points, NCRs, CAPA, risk assessments, permits, incidents, environmental controls, statutory evidence, audits and assurance reviews.

## Commissioning, completion and handover

NuBlox manages commissioning plans, systems, tests, witness requirements, results, defects/retests, completion evidence, certificates, as-built information, O&M information, training, warranties, asset information, handover packages and acceptance.

## Asset, facilities and service operations

The same asset identity continues into condition monitoring, maintenance strategy, plans, work orders, service requests, failures, inspections, parts, warranty/aftercare, service history and lifecycle replacement.

## Cross-functional truth

The delivery lifecycle is successful when the same underlying customer, project, contract, supplier, item, asset, deliverable, financial consequence and evidence can be followed from origin to final outcome.
